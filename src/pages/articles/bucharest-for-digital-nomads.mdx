## How affordable is Bucharest?

When I first came here in 2023, it was because it was the capital city of one of the cheapest cities in the EU. And while it is still relatively cheap by European levels, prices have jumped noticeably in the three years since.

For example at Level Up, one of Bucharest's vegan restaurants, a burger with salad and fries that cost 38 lei in 2023 costs 60 lei today. I looked into staying in my 2023 Airbnb again for this most recent visit, but quickly looked elsewhere because the price had doubled. You'll find similar price increases across the board.

So while it's still comparatively good value if you want to stay in a major EU city, it's no longer as cheap as its reputation would have you believe.

## Paying for things in Bucharest

The currency of Romania is the Romanian leu, which has the plural form lei and is what you'll commonly see prices suffixed with. In international currency the abbreviation is RON.

Card payments including Apple Pay are accepted basically everywhere. That includes being able to pay for metro tickets using your phone.

During my first visit in 2023 a small number of businesses were still cash-only - I specifically remember having to pay for my gym membership in cash. During my latest two visits I didn't have to use cash once. Everything that I paid for was with Apple Pay.

## What language is spoken in Bucharest?

Romanian.

## Is it easy to get by with English in Bucharest?

One advantage that Romania has over many of its neighbours is that it uses the Roman alphabet. Even if you don't know a word of Romanian, which I don't, it comes across to me as a mish-mash of other European languages, so if you speak English and have only a rudimentary knowledge of French and Spanish, you might find that you can understand more written Romanian than you'd expect.

If you go to the supermarket and see 'produse vegane', I probably don't need to tell you that indicates the vegan section. Or if you see 'Acces Parcare' and 'Nu blocati accesul în parcare', it won't take you too long to understand that means 'car park access' and 'don't block access to the car park'. 'Ceai verde' means green tea, 'case de bilete' means ticket office, and I'll give you one guess how to say 'carp' in Romanian.

This doesn't mean that you'll be able to understand everything that you read - you won't, not even close, but you won't feel as lost as you do in other countries in this region that don't use the Roman alphabet.

In terms of how widely English is spoken, you shouldn't expect it from everyone, but most people that you're likely to deal with, for example those working in restaurants or in barber shops, typically speak some English. I find it pretty easy to get by here.

## How is the Internet in Bucharest?

Lightning-fast and reliable.

In my first room here in 2023, the download speeds were over 600mbps and upload speeds over 500mbps. In my second room in 2025 I had 400mbps download and 425mbps upload speeds. I've had no reliability issues, and haven't been anywhere in the city without strong mobile coverage.

## Getting in and out of Bucharest

If you're arriving or departing Bucharest by air, you'll likely be doing so at Henri Coandă International Airport, Bucharest's main airport, located 16.5km north of the city centre. The most hassle-free way of getting there from the city centre is on the airport train, which runs 24 hours per day, departing in each direction between the airport and <ExternalLink url='https://maps.app.goo.gl/6LxR7ufhRRZ7poRX7'>Gara de Nord</ExternalLink> every 40 minutes. The journey takes 20 minutes and tickets cost 6.50 lei.

While it's possible to buy tickets at the ticket office before boarding, it's not necessary.

The first time I took this train in 2025, English-speaking conductors walked down the train, taking payment and printing tickets for passengers. By 2026 they'd been replaced by a contactless payment machine that you scan your debit card or Apple Pay against when you get on. A non-English speaking inspector did still walk down the train checking that everyone had done so.

Gara de Nord is also likely to be the station you arrive at in Bucharest if you come by train, and it's on lines 1 and 4 of the metro, so taking the underground from here to wherever you're staying is easy.

There's also an airport bus - #100. I took this bus to the airport at the end of my 2026 stay. Just like the train, there's a POS machine on board to scan your debit card, but this was the only time through my entire stay in Bucharest where paying with Apple Pay failed. Other people have reported that rather ironically, the bus to the international airport doesn't accept foreign cards. No inspectors boarded so I got a free ride, but to be safe you might want to buy a ticket before getting on.

If arriving or departing by bus, there are two main bus terminals. <ExternalLink url='https://maps.app.goo.gl/sa1vwkV6kBtCnXMH8'>Autogara Militari</ExternalLink> is west of the city centre, but an easy walk from Păcii metro station on line 3. When I took a bus to Sofia in 2023, this is where it departed from. I've never had to go to <ExternalLink url='https://maps.app.goo.gl/V5sQmoAwpWeunxkr9'>Autogara Filaret</ExternalLink>, but it also serves long-distance buses.

## Getting around Bucharest

Bucharest has a metro, buses, trams and trolleys that get to all corners of the city. Google Maps works really well at telling you the quickest way to get anywhere.

On the metro, your ticket is valid until you leave the network. On buses, trams and trolleys, your ticket is valid for 90 minutes, no matter how many rides you take.

You can always pay with contactless, however note that not all machines accept contactless.

In my experience on the trams, they have ticket machines at every door, however only one of these accepts contactless payment, so if one machine doesn't, go and look for a more modern machine. At Anghel Saligny metro station, only one of the ticket gates accepts contactless - the others don't. I only knew that because the security guard directed me to the right one. Wouldn't have had a clue otherwise.

So while I've always been able to pay by contactless when travelling on public transport, save for the airport bus, not all tickets machines are compatible.

## Is Bucharest safe?

I've seen nothing to make me think that it's not, and that includes wandering the streets drunk in the middle of the night.

The one thing to be aware of is that Bucharest city centre has a very evident problem with drug addiction. You will be asked for money a lot, and you'll see a lot of people clearly off their tits in a non-threatening way.

Outside of the city centre this is not an issue.

## Who is Bucharest suitable for?

Being in the EU and still being one of the cheapest EU countries, Bucharest is most suited to EU passport holders on a budget. As of 2025 Romania is a fully-fledged member of the Schengen Area, meaning that it's no longer suitable for nomads looking for a Schengen cool-off.

It's got a lot of green space, so it's very suited to those who like to get outside a lot, and it's a pretty vegan-friendly city.

The social scene here is a little underwhelming. While it does exist, it's not as vibrant as in other places, so it's perhaps not suited for nomads who're very socially-needy.

So basically it's suitable for poor, vegan, introverts who likes the outdoors and have an EU passport, which is why I keep on coming back.

## Is the tap water drinkable in Bucharest?

Very much so.

## Things I don't like about Bucharest

### Smoking

Bucharest hasn't escaped the plague that infects the rest of Eastern Europe, with an uncomfortably high proportion of the population doing what they can to give everyone else cancer.

Men smoke. Women smoke. Children smoke.

I'm not joking, I saw a school trip and half the kids had cigarettes. Smoking is at least banned indoors, but good luck trying to breathe clean air consistently while walking down the street.

### Drug addiction

Mainly in the city centre you'll see a lot of evidence of drug addiction. People trying to fly, that kind of thing.

That means that firstly, you're going to have a lot of people ask you for money. And secondly, there are a lot of people behaving erratically. I've never seen any violence directed towards other people, but I did see one guy get into a fight with a trash can once. And he won. That kind of unpredictability can put you a little on edge.

### Emergency messages

The Bucharest authorities are way, way too trigger happy with those emergency messages that get sent to your phone, where even if it's on silent and on do not disturb, it'll start wailing at you. On multiple occasions I've been in the middle of video calls with my phone on do not disturb when they've been sent, which is pretty embarrassing when you're on a work call.

If these messages were being sent for legitimate reasons I wouldn't mind, but the first one I received during my second stay in Bucharest was because someone had misplaced their child.

Ok, great. Thanks for telling me.

There was another one when everyone was told to seek emergency shelter from an incoming storm. It didn't even rain. Multiple others came telling me about a heatwave. It wasn't even that hot.

My guess is that the authorities are so paranoid that they'll face criticism if anything happens and they didn't send out an emergency message, that they just send them out for anything, which can get very annoying annoying.

The social scene for digital nomads in Bucharest isn't completely dead, but it's certainly a bit sickly.

## Running in Bucharest

Like many cities in Europe, Bucharest is very running-friendly. While the pavements are quiet enough and the pollution insignificant enough that you can comfortably run along the roads here, there's really no need as there are some really great parks in Bucharest.

### Tineretului Park

<ExternalLink url='https://maps.app.goo.gl/AgPQKS9SuYQrxYSJ6'>Tineretului Park</ExternalLink> is a big park south of the city centre. During my second visit to Bucharest I was staying intentionally close by and I came here pretty much everyday to either walk, run, or just get outside and get some sun.

The most obvious running route here is to do laps of the lake, one circumference of which is 2.8km. It's got a wide path all the way around, shared by runners, cyclists, walkers, rollerbladers and electric scooters. Apart from one small corner that's home to a lot of birds that stay off the path unless some genius parents decide to take their kids to feed them, there are no real bottlenecks, so you can comfortably run in either direction.

At the north-eastern tip of the lake you'll find a drinking fountain, with a few others dotted around. Even if you don't want to run, this is a great place for a lunchtime walk. There are hundreds of benches all over the park if you want to relax, and quite well hidden on top of a hill is a <ExternalLink url='https://maps.app.goo.gl/mhBRpvGwHoEn455F9'>calisthenics area</ExternalLink> where you can come to do some pull-ups.

The one obvious and expected deficiency in this park is the number of people that smoke, where they make every effort to annoy as many people that are exercising as possible. Given the size of the park, it isn't as bad as in some other parks in Eastern Europe.

### Văcărești Natural Park

<ExternalLink url='https://maps.app.goo.gl/vBE64sfmxc3QRcgQ8'>Văcărești Natural Park</ExternalLink> is probably my favourite place in Bucharest. It's a slightly peculiar place, because it's surrounded by this big, concrete bowl, and as typically happens when you build something out of concrete, people graffitied all over it. That makes it slightly unwelcoming, made even more so by how inconspicuous the entrances are.

It perhaps explains why there's barely ever anyone else here, but I love it. The presumed intention of the bowl is to keep all pollution, including noise pollution, out of the park, which it does very effectively. The park is bordered on two sides by a main road, but once you're inside you wouldn't know it. It's a very peaceful, untamed area of parkland within easy reach of Bucharest city centre. For walking or running, I suggest having it on your list of places to come in Bucharest.

If you're running here you've got two options. You can either go down into the park where there are some trails that are suitable. Or you can run around the edge of the bowl. I prefer the latter.

The top of the bowl has a path that goes all the way around it, with one lap being 5.14km. The downside of this is that being on top of the bowl you're not protected from the noise of the nearby roads, and you're also out in the open with little shade.

As Văcărești is fairly close to Tineretului Park, you can easily combine them into a longer run, the only downside being that to get between the parks you have to cross a couple of main roads that can take a little while.

### King Mihai I Park

<ExternalLink url='https://maps.app.goo.gl/FWoP5io8EQRJmvn6A'>King Mihai I Park</ExternalLink>, north of the city centre, is where I did all of my runs in my 2026 stay in Bucharest.

The park itself isn't that big, but what makes it great for running is that it leads down to the riverside, which has a running-suitable path along both sides, so you can use a couple of bridges to make it into a loop.

While it's very pleasant for slower runs, I'd caution against using it for faster, or timed runs, because it's quite a narrow path with some bottlenecks, shared between runners, walkers, cyclsts and more in both directions. There are also restaurants along part of the route. They have people congregating outside smoking sometimes, or vehicles stopped outside making deliveries, making the already narrow path single file.

If you try to do a timed run here, you'll likely find yourself stressed-out by one of these, or some old person walking their dog on a lead, walking along the very right of the path, while their dog walks along the left, while you're 6km into a fast run and have to struggle to gather enough breath just to tell him to get out the fucking way.

It's much more relaxing to run slowly here.

If you start at the main park entrance, go down to the river and do a loop, it comes to about 7km, but as long as you don't mind stopping at a couple of crossings, the roads to get to the park are very running friendly, so I'd usually run from my room in Piața Victoriei, making it closer to 12km.

## Vegan-friendly restaurants in Bucharest

Bucharest lacks a little bit in fully vegan restaurants. There are only a couple in the city centre. However, being vegan here is still very easy because most restaurants have something vegan on the menu.

The staff are really friendly, they speak English, and there's an English copy of the menu. It's all good with one notable caveat: They only open at 2pm. They've got to be the only restaurant in the world that opens right after the lunch rush.

This place is huge to a fault, because one of my only two grivances about it, is that the wifi barely reaches the bed. Annoyingly so in fact. It does work there, but only sometimes.

Sometimes you can lie in bed and stream video without issue. Other times you can't even load a webpage, and that's more annoying than having no wifi at all.

I was never able to locate the router, but there's a locked cabinet in the storgae cupboard, so I'd assume it's in there, but that in itself is stupid, because if the router was out in the open it'd have two fewer walls to go through and would likely work more reliably when you're in bed.

My only other grievance, is that to open and look the front door, you have to use an app on your phone. There is no physical key.

That's annoying enough - I'd like to just have a key. But worse is that this motorised lock often jams when opening the door, and I ultimately found that you have to pull on the front door with exactly the right amount of pressure each time to get the door open.

Don't pull hard enough, the lock jams. Pull too hard, the lock jams. On the worst occasion, which was a very hot day so presumably the mechanism had expanded, it took me eight attempts before I finally got the front door open. I'm really not sure why that's better than just having a key.

Besides those two things, I loved this room.

The bed was really comfortable, there's loads of space for exercise in the living room once you pull the rug out the way, the kitchen's really well equipped, it's the first time in my life that I've ever had a study, and the bedroom, living room, and study each have their own air conditioners.

And I've not even mentioned what's perhaps my favourite thing about this room - the location. It's a 20-25 minute walk to King Mihai I Park, which is where I did all my runs while I was here. Sara Green (vegan restaurant) is less than 10 minutes walk. There are multiple branches of Mega Image that are easily accessible, and public transport links are very nearby.

Piața Victoriei metro station is less than a 10 minute walk. The buses that stop right outside Piața Victoriei include the #100 (the airport bus), so you can be at Henri Coandă airport within 35 minutes of closing the front door if you time it right. But probably the most useful from my perspective, was that the #1 tram stops right outside the room, and that takes you within easy walking distance of Decathlon in Obor, and Bebe Tei in Vitan.

The wifi not realibly reaching the bed was a pain, and the front door lock was definitely an annoyance, but I would stay here again in an instant. I loved it here.

## Do I want to come back to Bucharest again?

I absolutely love my life as a digital nomad and I'll live out the rest of my days like this if I can. Unfortunately, at some point reality is going to bring me back down to earth.

Whether it's for financial reasons, for medical reasons, because I can't find remote work anymore, or some other reason altogether, a day is going to come when I have to settle down and live like a normal person again. And when that time comes, Bucharest is going to be at the top of my list. For what I look for in a city, it has it all.

It's affordable, it's very vegan-friendly, it's got loads of open, green space, it's got low pollution levels, the people are mostly friendly, and it's in the EU, meaning I can stay here forever without any red tape.

I hope that day is a long way off, but in the meantime I will for sure be back in Bucharest again. I love it here.
