## Why did I come to Cluj?

In 2023 I was in Bucharest. Drunk. And I met a guy, also drunk, who told me that as well as Bucharest, I should make sure to visit Cluj while I was in Romania.

For some reason that conversation stuck with me, which is weird because normally I don't remember anything the next day. Yet for some reasons, three years later it still stuck in my mind.

Now I know what you're thinking, _"why did it take you three years to come to Cluj then?"_

It wasn't for a lack of trying that I didn't come here earlier. For 2023, 2024 and 2025 Cluj was on my radar for the European part of my annual cycle, but two key factors for me when I go anywhere, are the cost, and the weather. I don't like cold weather, and I don't like spending money unnecessarily. And the climate of Cluj precludes it from being a place that I'd want to be from October to May. Even in September when I was here, I found it a bit uncomfortable. That means there's only really a four-month spell each year when I'd be willing to come here. The other problem is that for three of those months, accommodation in Cluj is really expensive.

I was never quite sure why - I think it's a combination simply being the high season, while also having some big festivals. But the room I stayed in through September, had I instead stayed in August cost about 3x the price. Which means there's a very narrow window of about one month per year where Cluj has a suitably temperate climate and cost.

### Did it live up to my expectations?

It's very pretty and has a very comfortable vibe, in the city centre at least, which makes it a nice place to be. I really enjoyed every time I went out running here, or just went out for a wander. But beyond being pleasant, nothing really stands out. I wouldn't really describe it as exceptional in any of the things I look for when I decide to go somewhere.

## Getting in and out of Cluj

I arrived in Cluj by Flixbus, which drops passengers off at <ExternalLink url='https://maps.app.goo.gl/yrkgbTnnPavNqqG1A'>airport park and ride</ExternalLink>. If you're arriving from the west of the country, as I was, that's a bit frustrating because the bus drives through the city centre only to drop you 6km outside of it.

On the plus side, as you'll be arriving at the airport whether you come by bus or plane, there's less to learn. The airport's on a main road with local bus stops all along it. The number 5 and 8 buses run frequently and get you into the city centre in about 25 minutes. You can pay on board with contactless, including Apple Pay, for 3.50 lei.

## Getting around Cluj

Buses and trams run all over Cluj, and on all of them you can pay the 3.50 lei with contactless. I used Google Maps to tell me which buses/trams to take, with moderate reliability.

## How affordable is Cluj?

It's affordable outside of the peak season, but accommodation prices are hugely inflated in the summer months. And I found the restaurant scene to be a little bit uppity. There are less reasonably priced places to get food here than elsehwere in Romania, instead favouring higher prices because they put a glass upside down on the table before you arrive.

If you can get an affordable room and with a good kitchen, the other key things like groceries and public transport a very reasonable.

## Paying for things in Cluj

I didn't have to buy a single thing in Cluj that I didn't pay for with Apple Pay.
