Timișoara is Romania's third largest city. It's so far in the west of the country that it's closer to Belgrade, Budapest, Sarajevo, Pristina and Sofia than it is to Bucharest.

I'm not a big architecure guy so I'm not sure about all the fancy words, but there are some cool as fuck buildings here, particularly in the city centre. Yet for reasons uncertain, it just doesn't really attract tourists. The city centre almost feels like a return to Covid it's so empty, but I mean that in a good way. I hate tourists.

I quite like that to be honest, and another benefit is how clean the city centre is, because with no people, there are no people throwing their litter on the floor.

All of this is great, but none of it really affects if you should come here or not. What is Timișoara actually like for a digital nomad?

## Getting in and out of Timișoara

If arriving by plane as I did, you'll be arriving at the <ExternalLink url='https://maps.app.goo.gl/3doqiHmagdSG4ymo8'>Timișoara International Airport</ExternalLink>, north-east of the city centre. Getting to the city centre by public transport from here, your only option is the public bus - either the E4 or the E4b.

The bus stop is right outside the arrivals area, but it's a little inconspicuous - it's just a couple of timetable signs attached to a pole. The first E4 leaves at 4:55am (or 6:15am on holidays), and they run until 10:59pm (or 10:07pm on holidays), with 1-2 every hour. The E4b is less frequent, but runs until 11:25pm (except on holidays).

I just missed an E4b when I arrived, so took the next E4 to its terminal stop, <ExternalLink url='https://maps.app.goo.gl/WmcMUPU7A8pQz7y38'>Bastion</ExternalLink>. The fare was 5 lei, I was able to pay on board with contactless, there were only two other passengers for the entire ride, and it took 15-20 minutes.

The E4b goes a slightly longer route and ends up someone near the Gara Timișoara Nord train station, although I'm uncertain on the details.

When I left Timișoara it was on a Flixbus. That departed from <ExternalLink url='https://maps.app.goo.gl/DZ5hRpKCcY6hs9Qa6'>Autogara Normandia</ExternalLink>, which is basically just a car park with a couple of ticket offices made out of shipping containers. Getting here is easy as there's a tram stop right outside.

## Getting around Timișoara

Taking public transport in Timișoara is easy. Apart from on my final day here where the onboard POS machines were bugging-out and not accepting contactless payments (which was catching a lot of people out), I was always able to pay on board with Apple Pay. And even if you can't, I didn't see a ticket inspector through my whole stay.

The easiest way to figure-out which buses/trams to get is with Google Maps, which I found to be 99% reliable, except one time telling me to take a bus from a stop that didn't exist. You can also checkout <ExternalLink url='https://stpt.ro/en/'>Transport Public Timișoara</ExternalLink> which has information about all the lines, including a frustratingly well hidden <ExternalLink url='https://live.stpt.ro/'>travel planner</ExternalLink>.

Every journey I took cost 5 lei.

## How affordable is Timișoara?

My very comfortable apartment in Timișoara cost me the equivalent of about 233 lei per night, a plate of food in a restaurant will cost around 40 lei, one trip on public transport is 5 lei, and I spent around 350 lei per week on groceries, although you could easily spend less.

It's a little cheaper than Bucharest, but not by much.

## Paying for things in Timișoara

I didn't buy a single thing in Timișoara that I didn't pay for with Apple Pay. Contactless is basically universal.

## What language is spoken in Timișoara?

Romanian.

## Is it easy to get by with English in Timișoara?

Relatively, yes. Most people that I interacted with spoke some basic English, but certainly don't expect it.

## How is the Internet in Timișoara?

I had no issues. The wifi in my room was 100% reliable, scoring 583mbps download and 486mbps upload speeds on the speed test that I did. I had no realiability issues mobile data throughout town.

## Is Timișoara safe?

I'd say so, yes.

There is a lot of evidence of homelessness, and an expectant amount of crazies that usually brings. But I didn't experience anything remotely threatening during my stay.

## Who is Timișoara suitable for?

I had zero social life while I was here.

I'm pretty introverted, and don't do well at meeting people outside of organised events such as Meetup, of which there are none here. So my time here was very isolated.

I enjoy that, but if you do not, then beware that meeting new people here isn't that easy.

The city is architecturally beautiful, and feels quite big and open, so it's a pleasant place to be outdoor, but I'd say there's not too much here to get excited about.

So if you like nice buildings, and you like being outside, but you don't like people, Timișoara might be for you.

## Is the tap water drinkable in Timișoara?

I hope so.

## Things I don't like about Timișoara

Apart from the things already mentioned - the lack of a social life, the high levels of homelessness, then my only other frustration here was the same as in every eastern European city: Smoking. People smoke here. A lot.

And while it is limited to outdoor areas thankfully, I'm convinced that smokers intentionally seek-out the places to smoke that will cause the maximum annoyance to others.

Oh, there's a nice park where people are exercising and children are playing? That's the perfect place for me to smoke.

## Where to run in Timișoara

Honestly, anywhere.

It's such a big, open, peaceful city, that you can run, even in the city centre without issue, so long as you don't mind waiting to cross the road sometimes.

Throughout my stay, I was only doing two kinds of runs - zone 2 runs and fast timed runs, neither of which are conducive to pedestrian crossings, so all of my runs in Timișoara came in the <ExternalLink url='https://maps.app.goo.gl/uvTrCqdM2Wi6TrcL8'>Botanic Park</ExternalLink>, for the sole reason that it was a two-minute walk from my room.

What makes it a botanic park, and not a normal park I'm not sure, because it looked just like a normal park to me. It's fairly small, one lap being 800-900m, depending on your exact route.

Being so close to my room, I came here almost every day that I was in Timișoara, whether to run or to walk or to just get outside, and it's quite fascinating how different it gets at different times of day. Earlier in the day it can attract a lot of drugged-up hobos, so can eel like a real life version of Zombies, Run! By the evening it's basically turned into a dirt track for kids and teens on bicycles and electric scooters.

For running, it's pretty unexciting, but if you need a place to do loops without crossing any roads, then it is that.

## Vegan-friendly restaurants in Timișoara

I mostly cooked in Timișoara so didn't get around to too many restaurants here, but it's a pretty easy place to be vegan if you do want to eat out.

One thing I learned while here, which is slightly concerning seeing as this is my third time in Romania, as that 'de post' essentially means vegan - it means that food contains no meat, dairy or eggs. So look out for eateries that advertise 'de post'. Once you know that, you realise that a lot of places are vegan friendly.

By far my favourite restaurant of the places that I did go was <ExternalLink url='https://maps.app.goo.gl/tTc6kq8hDRnnwyYZ7'>Biofresh</ExternalLink> in the city centre. It's listed as a vegetarian restaurant, but everything I asked about on the menu was confirmed vegan. It's freshly-cooked, healthy food.

Another place that I went to multiple times was <ExternalLink url='https://maps.app.goo.gl/1qX1X1jYXtATz6237'>Falafel King</ExternalLink> in the Iulius Town food court. It's a falafel-based restaurant if you couldn't tell, and they have an entire 'de post' menu. The food is decent - it's vegan at least, but the people making it do like to be a bit over-enthusiastic with the sauce, so temper your expectations.

## Getting vegan groceries in Timișoara

All roughly equal distance from my room were a branch of <ExternalLink url='https://maps.app.goo.gl/FJkTkpdgLwDatLMw6'>Lidl</ExternalLink>, a smallish <ExternalLink url='https://maps.app.goo.gl/fui6dDC5rFXoAQhv9'>Carrefour</ExternalLink>, and a big <ExternalLink url='https://maps.app.goo.gl/pNmhMwh6d2TGtteF6'>Mega Image</ExternalLink>. Lidl was the best for fruit and for mixed nuts, but not much else. Carrefour had the best quality and best value soy milk, the biggest range of tofu and of rice, and was the best place for more specific nuts and seeds. Everything else I needed I got at Mega Image, which is always very vegan-friendly.

Vegan grocery shopping in Timișoara is pretty easy.

## Getting vegan supplements in Timișoara

Coming here from Bucharest where I'd stocked-up, I didn't have to buy too many supplmenents in Timișoara.

The one thing that I did need was vegan protein powder, and the best that I found was in <ExternalLink url='https://maps.app.goo.gl/pj5f9zfvqX9m2Py49'>Vitabolic</ExternalLink>, which is a local brand with corresponding prices. 750g of really good vegan protein powder only cost 80 lei. They had a tonne of other stuff on their shelves, so worth coming here for a look. The staff speak English.

The only other supplement I bought was a opportune purchase of creatine in <ExternalLink url='https://maps.app.goo.gl/WoiPs78tjUSQajM57'>Decathlon</ExternalLink>. They also sell vegan protein powder, but it's almost twice the price of that at Vitabolic, costing 150 lei for 750g.

Very frustatingly I stumbled across a shop called Enigma Plant in <ExternalLink url='https://maps.app.goo.gl/yum8G8bguaPNchQY7'>Iulius Town</ExternalLink> opposite Primark, on my final day in Timișoara. I had a quick look round and it looked like a shop where, had I not been leaving the next morning, would have had some useful things.

The name is misleading as it's not all plant supplements - they sell whey protein, for example, but it seemed to be mainly plant-based supplements and health foods.

This is a great place - I really enjoyed my stay here.

It's slightly north of the city centre, but it's an easy walk to get in. Instead you're across the street from Iulius Town shopping mall, which includes Starbucks, Primark and a food court. The bedroom faces northeastish, so gets plenty of daylight in the mornings, but streaming onto the wall opposite the bed, which is perfect. My circadian rhythm loved being here. The kitchen, I'll be honest, felt a little lacking. It had all the utensils I needed, but it didn't have a teatowel, or any especially impressive knives. There's a dining table with four chairs in the kitchen, but one thing that's missing is a blind on the kitchen window, because by abouy 11am each morning, the sun streams in and there's little escape from it if you're sitting at the table. The bathroom was equipped with everything I needed.

One peculiarity about this apartment is that there's only one AC unit, and it's in the hallway. I actually liked that, because I didn't have to switch which one was turned-on whenever I went into a different room - I just kept the same one on all day, and it kept the kitchen, the bathroom, the living room and the bedroom equally cool, but it was a little bit odd.

Timișoara has its share of boy-racer types with their loud engines and small penisis, and this room is also nearby to a hospital, so ambulances go past with their sirens blaring all day.

In the area outside the apartment is some benches that residents will congregate at every day, and one of these has a very yappy dog who will bark at any other dog walking past. So if you are sensitive to noise, be prepared to have the windows shut all day.

I am not, I've lived in loud places before so the noise doesn't really bother me. However there were two occasions while staying here that even I was disturbed.

I'm pretty sure that the room downstairs is also an Airbnb, because it would here voices down there, then it'd be quiet for a few days, then I'd hear different voices. On two occasions I got woken up by drunken idiots in the room downstairs.

The first of these was on a Thursday night, when at about 2am a group of lads started loudly and drunkenly singing along to some music that was playing in a language I couldn't identify. This went on until about 4am. The next week on the Friday there was no music, just one guy who was drunk enough that he felt like he needed to shout everything.

Other than this there were no issues, and I actually really enjoyed my stay in this room. It was worth the couple of noisy parties for the 33 other nights here where I slept well and got woken each morning with the sun streaming into the room, and to be so close to a park. I would gladly stay again.

## Do I want to come back to Timișoara again?

Timișoara goes onto my ever growing list of places that aren't very exciting, but that are very pleasant and affordable, and you need such places as a digital nomad.

People often mistake being a digital nomad for being a long holiday, asking you _"what do you do there?"_ whenever you go somewhere. And for me the answer more often than not, is exactly the same things that I do everywhere else I go.

Life cannot be one long holiday - you need places to recharge and focus on yourself, and to do that you need places like Timișoara. So do I want to come back to Timișoara again?

Not especially, but I gladly would.

It's hard to be excited to return to somewhere that's not very exciting, but if I need another month sometime in the future to just get away from everything, Timișoara is as good a place as anywhere else that I've been, so I'd happily come back and not talk to anyone again.
